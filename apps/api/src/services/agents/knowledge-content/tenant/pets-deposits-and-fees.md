title: Pets: what your lease says, and what you might be charged
---

**Whether you can keep a pet is a term of your signed lease, not a GAM setting.**
There's no platform-wide pet policy and no switch your landlord flips — it's
written into the lease you signed, the same as your rent and your term. So the
honest answer to "am I allowed a pet?" is whatever your own lease says, and that
document is the only place to get it.

Ask and the assistant will read your lease and tell you what's actually in it.
If the lease is silent on pets, it's silent — that isn't permission and it isn't
a refusal, and it's worth asking your landlord directly before bringing an
animal home.

## The two charges you may see

Where a lease does allow a pet, it can carry either or both of:

- A **pet deposit** — held like your security deposit and dealt with the same
  way at move-out. It's refundable in principle; what actually comes back
  depends on the condition of the unit, exactly as with the main deposit.
- A **pet fee** — a charge rather than a deposit. It isn't held on your behalf
  and it doesn't come back.

They are different things and a lease can name one, both, or neither, at
whatever amounts the landlord set. Your own figures are on your lease — ask for
them rather than assuming a typical amount, because there isn't one.

## If a pet is causing a problem

A neighbour's animal — barking, mess, an animal somewhere it shouldn't be — is a
complaint, filed under **pets**, and goes to your landlord. See *Reporting a
problem with a neighbour, a pet, parking, or the property*.

## Assistance animals

Service animals and assistance animals are treated differently from pets under
law in most places, and the rules vary by where you live. GAM doesn't decide
this and the assistant won't guess at it. If this applies to you, raise it with
your landlord directly, and ask GAM to put you in touch with a person if you'd
rather not do that alone.
