title: Unit inspections: move-in, move-out, and what your signature means
---
GAM gives your landlord a structured way to document the condition of your unit, and gives you a clear record of what was found. The inspections you'll take part in are move-in (done around the start of your tenancy), move-out (done when you leave), and periodic (a routine check during your stay). (Your landlord may also run an internal turnover check between tenancies — that one isn't something you sign.)

For your **move-in** inspection, your landlord walks through the unit area by area. Each item, like a wall, an appliance, or flooring, gets a condition: good, fair, damaged, missing, or not applicable, with photos and notes to back it up. When the documentation is ready, you'll get a notification asking you to review and sign — your signature applies to the move-in inspection only. **Periodic** inspections work differently: you complete those yourself in the portal, photographing each area and submitting. **Move-out** inspections are conducted by your landlord's staff and don't require your signature.

What does signing mean? Your signature is your attestation that what you're seeing matches what's documented. It does not mean you agree the unit is perfect, and it does not waive anything; it confirms the record reflects what's actually there. Look over every item and photo carefully before you sign. If something looks wrong or is missing, contact your landlord through the platform before signing so it can be corrected, since items can't be edited once the inspection is finalized.

After you sign the move-in inspection, your landlord signs to finalize, and it is locked. At move-out, GAM compares your move-out inspection against your move-in record for the same items, so anything that got worse is documented side by side. Brand-new items not on the move-in list don't count against that comparison.

You can view all of your inspections and their results anytime in your tenant portal. How any documented damage affects deposits or charges depends on your lease and your local law, so review your lease for those details.
