scope: tenant
title: Logging work trade hours and how credits apply
---
Some properties offer work trade: you do agreed-upon work around the property, and the hours you put in reduce your monthly bill. Your landlord sets up the agreement — it lists your duties, the term, and your monthly hours target. You'll find it all under **Work Trade** in your portal's sidebar, where you log your hours and watch your credit build.

## How the credit works

Work trade is a percent model, not an hourly wage. Each approved hour covers 1/target of your total monthly bill — rent plus utilities plus fees. If your target is 80 hours, one hour covers 1.25% of the bill; if it's 100 hours, one hour covers 1%. A charge fully covered by your credit shows as settled with a "Covered by work-trade credit" note. The credit applies to rent first, then utilities, then fees.

Your agreement says which charges it covers. If something is excluded — propane, say — you're billed for that in full and your hours never buy it down.

## The month you work is the month you're paying for

You work during the month you're living there, and those hours pay for that month. Your invoice is issued at the start of the month as normal, but it **sits open while you work it off — and no late fees are charged on it**. At the end of the month, your approved hours for that month are applied to that month's bill.

You do not need to work a month ahead, and you're never asked to pay for a month before you've had the chance to work it.

## If you come up short, and if you work ahead

Falling short carries forward **in hours**, not dollars. On an 80-hour agreement, if you work 60, then 20 hours carry into next month — so next month you'd need 100 hours to be completely straight. Your portal shows both figures and the total, so you always know where you stand.

Working extra also carries forward. Hours beyond your target are banked and come off future months, and they never expire. They buy months, though — they never pay out as cash. No single month can be credited for more than that month's bill.

Your landlord decides how long a shortfall can carry before it's settled up. If the hours still aren't worked by then, the uncovered part of the bill is charged as an ordinary balance and the work-trade agreement ends. If that happens: it's **never charged a late fee**, and unlike rent, you can pay it down in any amount rather than all at once. Each carried hour is valued at the rate of the month it was owed for, so a later rent increase doesn't change what old hours are worth.

Your landlord can also end the agreement at any time, and any hours not completed are billed at that point on the same terms.

## Logging your hours

For each work session, submit the work date, the hours worked, and a short description of what you did — all three are required. Each entry starts as **pending** and only counts once your landlord **approves** it. A rejected entry doesn't count, and may include a reason. Your log shows every entry with its status, and your progress view shows approved hours against your target for the current month.

A practical tip: log hours as you work them and keep descriptions specific. Your landlord reviews each entry, and a clear description ("cleaned pool deck and skimmed pool, 2.5 hrs") gets approved faster than a vague one. Logging as you go also matters more than it used to — hours are applied at the end of the month they were worked, so an entry sitting unapproved when the month closes can't count toward it. If an entry sits pending or gets rejected and you're not sure why, talk to your landlord — they control approvals and the agreement terms.
