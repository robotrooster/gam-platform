scope: tenant
title: How your payments are applied to what you owe
---
When you pay through the Payments page, you don't pick which charge your money lands on — GAM applies every payment to your **oldest outstanding charge first**, by due date. This is automatic and the same for everyone.

## Why oldest-first matters

If you have an older unpaid balance, a new payment covers that balance before it touches current rent. So a payment made "for this month's rent" may actually settle last month's charge first, leaving part of the current month open. That's expected behavior, not a mistake — it keeps your oldest debts from aging while newer ones get paid around them.

The one exception is a **carried balance** — money you already owed your landlord before they moved onto GAM, or an amount carried over from an arrangement like work trade. Those sort **last**, not first, so a payment always brings your current charges up to date before any of it goes to the old debt.

## Rent is paid in full

Rent is **all-or-nothing**. One payment covers your entire outstanding rent balance for that lease; a payment for less than that is declined. This is platform-wide — it isn't something your landlord switches on or off, and there's no way to split this month's rent into two payments.

The reason is not arbitrary: a partial rent payment can restart your landlord's eviction clock, which helps nobody.

## What you *can* pay in part, and what you can pay ahead

**A carried balance can be paid down in any amount.** If you're carrying an old balance, you are not trapped behind it — you pay your current rent in full, and put whatever you like toward the older balance separately, whenever you can. If GAM ever refuses a payment for being short, the message tells you the figure your current charges need and says plainly that the carried balance can be paid separately.

One condition: if you rent more than one space from the same landlord, all of them have to be current before you can put money toward an old balance. Paying down old debt on one space while this month's rent sits open on another only earns you a late fee on the second one.

**You can also pay more than you owe.** Anything above your balance is kept on your account as credit for later bills — it stays your money in the meantime. There's no cap on how far ahead you can pay.

## More than one lease

If you have balances on more than one lease (say, an overlap while moving between units, or two different landlords), each lease is paid as its own charge — you'll see a separate Pay button per lease, plus a "Pay all" option to settle them together with one payment method.

## Credit on your account

Credit on your account — money you paid ahead, a credit from your landlord, or statutory interest on your deposit — pays a bill **by itself only when it covers the whole bill**. Then the bill is marked paid and you get a receipt saying it was paid with your account credit; nothing is charged to you.

When your credit is less than the bill, nothing happens on its own. Your bill shows in full, with "You have $X credit available" beside it, and when you pay you choose:

- **Use all $X** — your credit comes off and you pay the rest.
- **Save it for later** — you pay the whole bill and keep the credit.

If your credit covers everything you owe, you'll see **Pay with credit** instead, and nothing is charged.

A few things credit can't pay: a home or trailer payment, a GAM fee, or another landlord's charge on your bill. Credit tied to one lease pays only that lease, and if your landlord set a monthly limit on how much paid-ahead money a month may use, it stops there. Saved credit doesn't stop a late fee unless it covers the whole bill, so a bill you haven't paid can still be late.

**Autopay** has a setting, "Use my account credit first", that is off unless you turn it on. Off, autopay charges the whole bill and your credit waits for you. On, autopay uses all the credit it can and charges the rest.

## Seeing where every dollar went

Under "Payments you've made" on the Payments page, select any payment to expand its breakdown: each charge it was applied to, the due date, and the exact amount that landed there, plus any leftover amount that was kept on your account as credit. Your receipt lists the money you paid and any account credit applied as separate lines. If a payment fails, nothing is applied — the charges return to your outstanding balance and the breakdown says so.
