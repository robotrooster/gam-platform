title: How utility charges on your bill are calculated
---
Whether you pay for a given utility — water, gas, electric, sewer, or trash — comes from your signed lease. If your lease doesn't make you responsible for a utility, GAM never bills you for it.

## How the charge is calculated

Properties bill utilities one of three ways:

- **Individual meter (submeter):** your unit has its own meter. Usage is the difference between this month's reading and last month's, and the charge is that usage times the property's per-unit rate, plus any fixed base fee. The very first reading on a meter is just a baseline — it produces no bill.
- **Shared meter (RUBS):** one master meter serves several units, and the total is split among them by occupant count, square footage, bedrooms, or an equal split — including each unit's share of the base fee.
- **Landlord pays:** the landlord absorbs the master bill, and nothing appears on your account.

At some properties, sewer is billed off the water meter's reading at its own rate, as part of your water line item — there's no separate sewer meter. Your landlord may also configure a utility tax rate, which is calculated and recorded on each bill separately from the usage charge.

## Where it shows up

Utility charges ride your regular monthly invoice as their own "Utility" line items alongside rent — there's no separate utility payment to track, and you pay everything through the normal Pay Now flow. For sub-metered utilities, the bill records the beginning and ending meter readings and the usage that produced the charge, so you can see exactly where the number came from. A late meter reading simply rides your next invoice, and when you move out, any final utility usage is invoiced right away rather than waiting a cycle. If your property delivers propane, a fill can also appear as installments on your monthly invoices.
