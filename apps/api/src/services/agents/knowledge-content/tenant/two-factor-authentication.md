title: Setting up two-factor authentication
---
Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a one-time code to your sign-in, so a stolen password on its own isn't enough to get into your account. It's optional, and worth turning on — your account holds your payment methods and your lease.

## Setting it up

Start from your security settings, under Profile. There are three steps:

1. **Scan the QR code** with any authenticator app — Google Authenticator, Authy, 1Password and Bitwarden all work. If you can't scan it, there's a link to add the account by hand.
2. **Save your recovery codes.** You get 10 one-time codes, shown once and never retrievable again. Put them somewhere safe, like a password manager. You'll confirm you've saved them before you can finish.
3. **Enter the current 6-digit code** from your app to confirm. From your next sign-in on, 2FA is active.

## Signing in with it on

After your email and password you'll be asked for the 6-digit code from your app. That screen is short-lived — wait too long and you'll start the sign-in again.

## If you lose your authenticator

Use one of your saved recovery codes in place of the 6-digit code. Each one works exactly once. Once you're back in you can turn 2FA off (this needs your password) and re-enroll on a new device, which issues a fresh set of recovery codes.

If you've lost both the app and the recovery codes there is no self-service reset — that's an account-access matter and it goes to a person at GAM who can get you back in safely.

## Turning it off

Any time, from the same security settings. You'll re-enter your password to confirm — deliberately, so that someone who has gotten into an open session can't quietly strip your second factor off.
