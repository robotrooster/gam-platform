title: Getting paid: how your payouts work
---
When a tenant pays rent through GAM, the payment clears and then pays out to your connected bank account. GAM is the software platform that moves the money through Stripe.

Here's the rhythm. Card and bank (ACH) payments take time to clear before the funds are truly available — a bank debit sits with the bank for about four business days after your tenant sends it. Once funds clear they become your available balance.

Your payouts are driven by your rent roll, not by a calendar. GAM watches how much of your rent has come in each month and sends your money in batches as it arrives, so you are not left waiting on one or two late tenants:

- when **half** your occupied units have paid, a payout is scheduled
- when **90%** have paid, another one goes
- and there is a guaranteed sweep in the last full week of the month, which also covers a month where neither of those is reached

Each one is scheduled four business days out from the moment it is triggered, which is roughly how long a bank debit takes to clear. So if most of your tenants pay on the 1st through the 3rd, expect your first money around the 7th or 8th.

Every payout sends your **entire available balance**, not just the rent that triggered it. So if rent keeps arriving on the day the halfway mark is crossed, all of it goes out together. Some months you'll see two payouts instead of three simply because the rent came in fast — the total is the same, and you got it sooner.

Nightly and weekly stays work differently, because bookings arrive all month and there is no rent roll to measure a percentage against. Those sweep **weekly**. If you run both long-term leases and short stays, you get both, and they draw on the same balance.

Standard bank payouts are free and typically arrive in one to two business days after they're sent. If you'd rather not wait for the next scheduled run, you can also request an on-demand withdrawal of your available balance from your Disbursements page. An instant option is available where eligible — the instant transfer costs 2% ($5 minimum) for the faster rail, taken out of the transfer itself.

A couple of things worth knowing. The platform fee is $2 per occupied unit each month, with a $10 monthly minimum per payout account — you're billed whichever is higher; vacant units are never charged. When you first onboard, GAM doesn't start charging the platform fee until you go live — your first rent settles through the platform — so switching over from somewhere else doesn't mean paying two platforms at once. Whether the tenant or you cover the ACH fee is set per property; card fees are always the tenant's; GAM never absorbs that fee. Rent is collected electronically, but if a tenant pays you off-platform — cash, check, or money order — you or your staff can record that payment so their balance reflects it and it's marked settled. Recording an off-platform payment is **free** — no fee to you and none to the tenant. Nothing is run electronically, so there is no processing cost to pass on.

## Money that is still clearing

There is a gap between a tenant paying and the money being yours to spend. A
bank payment sits in the banking system for days **after** the tenant's account
was debited — they have paid, it has not landed.

That money is **not** overdue and those tenants are **not** behind. GAM counts
it as paid the moment it is sent, everywhere it matters: the tenant's own
balance drops, the late-fee engine stops, and it is excluded from anyone's
outstanding total. Ask what is still clearing, what is on its way, or what is in
your next payout so far, and you will get the figure and who it is from.

This is worth knowing because the two questions sound alike and are not. "Who
owes me money?" is about people who have not paid. "What is still clearing?" is
about people who have. Somebody mid-transfer belongs in the second answer and
never in the first.

## When a payment comes back

An ACH can be returned after it appeared to go through — insufficient funds, a
closed account, a stop payment. That money really is owed again, and it is
tracked separately from a tenant who simply never paid: one of them has already
tried. Ask who is behind and the two are reported apart, with the bank's reason
where it gave one, because they need different conversations.
