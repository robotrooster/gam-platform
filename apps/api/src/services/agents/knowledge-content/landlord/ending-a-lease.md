title: "Ending a Lease: Non-Renewal, Termination & Eviction Mode"
---
A lease can end several ways in GAM. Here's what each path does.

## The renewal decision

As a lease approaches its end date, the **Renewal Decision** flow gives you two cards:

- **Renew** — drafts a new lease from a template (the current lease's template is preselected) and opens it for signing immediately. The lease is the document: you type the new rent and dates directly into the lease, sign, and it goes to the tenant automatically. Tenant details, recurring fees, and the held deposit carry over — the deposit is never re-billed.
- **Don't Renew** — the lease ends on its end date and will not auto-renew. All tenants on the lease are notified immediately; on the end date the unit is vacated and the deposit-return process starts automatically. Check your local notice-period requirements — some jurisdictions require advance written notice.

## Early termination

Early termination is **tenant-initiated**: the tenant requests it in their portal, the early-termination fee auto-charges, and the lease flips to terminated. The fee comes from one of two places — an early-termination fee printed in the signed lease, or your default policy (a multiplier of monthly rent). If neither exists, there's no fee and the tenant can terminate without payment.

On the lease's **Early Termination** page you can see any request and its status, and — for good-faith cases like job relocation, mutual termination, or hardship — **waive the fee**, which terminates the lease immediately without charging the tenant. A waiver requires a written reason and is recorded with your name in the audit log. Move-out itself is separate: continue to the deposit return afterward.

## Eviction mode

Eviction mode is a per-unit toggle for when you're actively evicting. Turning it on **hard-blocks every tenant payment that would go to you** — bank and card alike, not just bank — and sets the unit's status to **suspended**. The two are coupled one-to-one, and suspended can't be set any other way. The tenant is told the unit is in eviction mode and to contact you; they cannot route around it by switching payment method. Balances the tenant owes GAM rather than you sit on separate rails and keep collecting. Turning it off restores the unit's prior status and resumes collections. A suspended unit stays on your rent roll: the tenant still owes rent under the lease. Eviction is legally fraught — GAM blocks payments for you, but knowing your local eviction rules (including whether accepting a payment affects your case) is your responsibility.
