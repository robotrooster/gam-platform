title: Recording expenses, and why your P&L looks light
---
GAM knows every dollar that came **in** — it collected it. It knows almost
nothing about what went **out**, because most of that never touches the
platform: the plumber you paid in cash, the insurance premium, the truck, the
lumber. Until you record those, your profit-and-loss is income minus the few
costs GAM can see, and it will read higher than the truth.

That is why the P&L says so on its face rather than calling the number "profit".
It is not a bug and it is not a data problem — it is a blank you fill in.

## Where the two screens are

Both sit under **Financials** in the sidebar.

- **Financials → Reports** is where the profit-and-loss lives. Income is broken
  out by rent, fees, utilities, late fees, home/trailer payments, register sales
  and stays, and other income; expenses by the GAM platform fee, maintenance,
  lot rent, and whatever you have entered yourself.
- **Financials → Expenses** is where you enter your own costs, and where the
  ones you have already recorded are listed.

## Recording one

On the Expenses page the form is **Log an expense or a bill** — not "add", if
you are hunting for it. It asks for:

- **Scope** — what the cost belongs to, so it lands against the right property
  or unit rather than the portfolio in general.
- **Category** — how it groups on the P&L. The list is your company's own:
  GAM starts you with the usual ones (repairs, utilities, insurance, taxes and
  so on), and you or your bookkeeper add, rename or archive them on
  **Bookkeeping → Categories**. A category can charge to any account of your
  chart; one charged to something that is not a cost (an owner draw, say) is
  real money out but stays off the P&L.
- **Vendor** — who you paid, picked from your vendors.
- **Amount** and **Date**.
- **Paid already** or **A bill to pay** — paid already asks how (from the
  bank, check, credit card, cash from the office, or paid personally by the
  owner). A bill to pay is the same
  record, still owed, with an optional due date and bill number; record its
  payment on the row when you pay it, in one go or in parts.
- **Description** — a line for what it actually was.
- **Receipt** — optional, and worth attaching. It is the difference between a
  number you can defend at tax time and one you cannot.

Saved expenses are listed on the same page under **All expenses**, **Bills to
pay** (what is still owed) or **Paid**, and the P&L on the Reports page picks
them up from there. Cash you paid is logged here by hand and never has to
match a bank line.

## Bills, the bank feed and repairs

Money going out is one record: an expense. A bill is simply an expense not paid
yet. When the bank feed shows money going out, **What did this pay?** offers
the open bills and checks you already logged first, plus GAM's own fee debit, a
refund check, a transfer to another of your accounts, or a payment on one of
your loans (split into principal, interest and escrow — check the split
against the lender's statement before you match it). Match it and the bill
is marked paid from the bank; only when nothing fits do you file it as a new
expense. **Undo this match** puts the line back to review and the bill back to
owed. A deposit that is one of your loans' money arriving shows **Is this
money from your loan?** instead, so the loan is never counted as income.

A finished repair with a cost can become a bill two ways, your choice on
**Bookkeeping → Books Settings**: an unpaid bill made by itself when the
repair is closed with its cost, or only when someone presses **Record as bill**
on Financials → Maintenance Costs. Either way the cost counts once on the P&L.

## Received, Charged and Owed

There is no switch: every report shows three money columns side by side, **Received** first.

- **Received** counts money on the day it arrived. Money a tenant pays ahead at your desk counts when it arrives, not again when it pays a later bill: one check for two months counts in full in the month you got it, and the next month shows $0 from that tenant. Money a tenant paid ahead online sits with GAM until it pays one of their bills, and counts that day. A refund or a payment taken back is a minus on its own day.
- **Charged** counts each bill in the month it was due, paid or not, less any credit you gave on it.
- **Owed** is what tenants still owed at the end of the period, every bill included, not only that period's.

A short line under each table says how the columns tie: what the money received was for, and how Owed got where it is. It reads: owed at the start, plus what was billed, less what was received against bills, credits you gave, work trade, money paid ahead that paid a bill and deposits used, plus any opening balances or corrections, equals owed at the end. It always adds up.

Each report's net is shown both ways, Received first: money received less the expenses you paid in the period, then money charged less the expenses dated in the period (a bill counts as charged on its date and as paid on the day you pay it). The same three columns are on the profit-and-loss, owner statements, the rent roll, the dashboard's "This month" card, the property reports and the monthly property report.

**A credit you give is never income.** A move-in special, a waived fee or any goodwill credit comes off: the charge it paid counts in Received only for what the tenant paid in money, and Charged is less the credit. Hours a tenant works off their rent follow the way you chose on **Bookkeeping → Books Settings**: the full rent with a labor cost beside it, or a rent discount.

Register sales, stays and pay links count on their own line, without sales tax or the card fee. A stay counts as income when the guest arrives (a stay of 30 nights or more, month by month); a deposit paid before then is held as a guest deposit. Money that never moved through GAM — laundry, interest, anything you file from your bank feed as other income — counts as "Other income". Sales tax, lodging tax and deposits are listed beside the total, never in it.

The property report leads with income by category: lot or space rent, each utility by type (electric, water, sewer, trash, propane and the rest), late fees, home payments, other fees, register sales, and stays and pay links. Each shows what was received, charged and owed side by side. Then come expenses, then the net. The unit-by-unit lists are below it, folded until you open them.

Once your books are set up (a start date on **Bookkeeping → Books Settings**), every report reads them. **Bookkeeping** also has the balance sheet, the trial balance for the company or any one property, and the general ledger: any account's lines with a running balance and the payment, bill or expense behind each one.

## What GAM already counts without being told

The platform fee, the card and bank fees you cover for tenants, card fees given
back with refunds, chargeback fees, a manager's share, payroll you run in GAM,
and maintenance and lot rent where those moved through GAM. Everything else is
yours to enter. If your P&L shows a suspiciously healthy net, the usual reason
is simply that a year of real costs has not been entered yet.
