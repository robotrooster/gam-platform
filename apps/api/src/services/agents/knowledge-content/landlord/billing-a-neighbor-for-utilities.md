scope: landlord
title: Billing a neighbor you supply utilities to
---
Sometimes you supply a utility to a space you don't rent out. Three trash cans serving the building next door, a submetered apartment on your electric, a neighbor on your water. You've probably been collecting for it in cash for years. GAM can bill it properly instead.

Go to **Utilities → Neighbor utilities → Add a space**. You name the space, say who pays for it, and set a billing day. It is not a rental unit: it doesn't count toward your occupancy, doesn't appear on your rent roll, is never bookable, and never shows as vacant. It exists so the utility charge has somewhere to live and so the person paying it has a way to log in and pay you.

**Nobody gets billed without agreeing.** Either the payer accepts an emailed invite, or — if you've had the arrangement for years and there's nothing to sign — you tick the box saying they've already agreed to pay for this. Until one of those is true, the charges pile up but no bill is issued. If you add a space and no invoice ever goes out, that box is almost always why.

After that you attach the space to your trash or electric or water the same way you attach any other space, and it bills on its own each cycle. **One bill per space, however many utilities.** Trash plus electric on the same neighbor is one invoice and one payment, not two.

The person paying gets a login of their own. They see what they owe, what the meter read, and they pay it like any tenant. There's also an "I'm moving out" button — that tells you to take a closing read. It doesn't end anything or wipe a balance.

Late fees follow whatever policy the property already has, stamped onto the arrangement when you create it. If your policy is a percentage of rent, it's calculated against the utilities instead, since there's no rent here.

The platform fee is **$2 per space per month**, the same as a unit, however many utilities that space is on — and it applies whether or not a meter is attached, because plenty of utilities are flat-rate or split rather than metered.

**If that neighbor's own landlord later joins GAM**, GAM notices and merges the billing on its own. The tenant next door starts seeing one bill — their rent and your utilities on a single document with one due date — and your utility money still comes to you. Nobody has to approve it and nothing changes for you; you just stop having a separate invoice for that space.
