---
GAM tracks your properties at the unit level, and occupancy matters both for managing tenants and for how your platform fee is calculated.

## Occupied vs. vacant

- An **occupied** unit has a current tenant.
- A **vacant** unit has no tenant.

Keeping unit occupancy accurate in GAM matters because the platform fee is based on it: you're charged **$2 per occupied unit per month**, **vacant units are never charged**, and there is a **$10/month minimum per payout account**. A unit counts as occupied for the month when it has an active lease or a paid month stay covering any part of the month, is owner-occupied, or is a space you bill utilities to — once, however many of those it has. Nightly and weekly stays are billed after the month ends — $2 per 30 booked nights on RV, camp, and boat sites, and 3% of booking revenue on furnished units. A space that fills up after the 1st is usually added to that month's bill the next weeknight evening; one filled in the last day or two of the month may start on the next month's bill instead. If a lease should have ended but is still active in GAM, end or terminate it so the unit stops counting.

## Why accurate units help

Accurate units and occupancy give you a clear picture of your portfolio, route the right maintenance requests and payments to the right tenants, and ensure your monthly fee reflects only the units actually in use.

## What GAM does and doesn't decide

GAM is the platform that records and tracks your units and tenants — GAM is not your landlord and does not own your properties. You decide lease terms, rent, who occupies each unit, and your own rules, within your lease and local law.

If you need help correcting a unit's occupancy status, reconciling your unit count against your bill, or you believe a fee doesn't match your actual occupancy, we can connect you with a human GAM Strategist to review it.
