---
scope: landlord
title: Charging a tenant for a violation, damage, or a replacement key
---
There's a difference between a fee that's part of the lease and a charge for something that happened on a Tuesday. Pet rent is a lease term. A fire-lane violation, a hole in the wall, a replacement key — those aren't in the lease, they just occurred.

You can add one of these against a tenant directly. Pick the tenant, choose what kind of charge it is, enter the amount, the date it happened, and **a reason, which is required**. The reason prints on their invoice next to the date, so the tenant sees what they're being charged for and when it happened rather than a mystery line item.

It rides their next invoice automatically along with rent. If you enter it after that month's invoice already went out, it lands on the following one rather than being lost.

Two things to know. These are **not** lease fees — they don't change the lease and they aren't a recurring term. And they are **not** creditable against a work-trade agreement; work trade covers agreed labor, not damage.

If what you actually want is a standing charge that recurs every month, that belongs in the lease as a lease fee, not here.
