title: What each team role can do, and limiting access by property
---
Every team member you add is scoped two ways: by what they can do (their role and permissions) and by which properties or units they can touch. Here's how to think about it.

What each role does:

- Property Manager — the broadest helper. With the right toggles, they can manage properties, units, tenants, and leases. You can also grant or withhold the maintenance-approval permission per manager. You decide which abilities are on; permissions you don't grant stay off.
- On-Site Manager — handles tasks at the properties you assign, without portfolio-wide reach.
- Maintenance — receives repair requests tenants file. You can limit a worker to specific job categories so they only see work that fits their trade. The twelve categories are general, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, appliance, landscape, pest control, cleaning, roofing, structural, pool and spa, and locksmith. Leave the categories empty and the worker sees every category within their property or unit scope.
- Bookkeeper — keeps one company's books: the properties you tick for them, the company-wide books if you tick that box, or both. For their properties they see the money: money in by type, money out, receipts and maintenance costs. Every bookkeeper does the full bookkeeping on what you tick: they add, correct, remove and pay expenses and bills, keep the company's categories, and with the boxes below file the bank lines going out and keep vendors, payroll and journal entries. There is one level for every bookkeeper, with no read-only setting. They never work a tenant's account: they never record a rent payment or give a credit, late fee or refund, and they never run the day-to-day, so you can give an accountant the books without handing over operational controls.

Limiting access by property or unit:

For managers and maintenance workers, you choose the boundary when you invite them and can change it later. An on-site manager's property is fixed when they're added — to move them, remove and re-invite them at the new property (their units and permissions can still be adjusted):

- All properties — full reach across everything you own.
- Specific properties — only the ones you select.
- Specific units — narrow it down to individual units within a property.

This is how you give a manager one building, assign a maintenance worker to a single park, or let someone help with just a handful of units. Adjust any team member's properties, units, or permissions from the Team page whenever roles change. When in doubt, grant the narrowest scope that lets them do the job, and widen it as needed.

Limiting a bookkeeper by property:

Every property is its own box. When you invite a bookkeeper, you pick the company whose books they keep and tick each property they should see. "Tick all" ticks every property the company owns today. There is also a separate box, "Company-wide: bank account, payouts and payroll". You can tick properties, Company-wide, or both. An invite with nothing ticked won't send. A company with no property yet can still have a bookkeeper if you tick Company-wide.

What each box gives:

- A property box: that property's money in and who paid it, its money out and receipts, its maintenance costs, its rent roll, and the Reports pages for it. They can add expenses for it.
- Company-wide: the company's bank account, payouts and payroll, plus the company's own GAM Books (its ledger, bank reconciliation, profit and loss, cash flow and owner statements) and the Close Month page — closing or reopening a month only when you allow it in Books Settings. These cover the whole company and can't be split by property, so a bookkeeper sees them only when you tick Company-wide. Ticking every property does not give them. The property money inside those reports still follows the property boxes: a bookkeeper sees only their ticked properties' rent and costs there, and a Company-wide-only bookkeeper sees the ledger, bank and payroll with no property's own money. Money going out on the bank feed is theirs to file. Money coming in is your choice for each bookkeeper: any deposit, only a deposit you marked "Not rent" (the default), or none at all.
- "May use the whole-company ledger": for a bookkeeper without Company-wide. They use the company's GAM Books ledger (chart of accounts, journal, bank reconciliation, vendors, payroll, tax, profit and loss, balance sheet), never the bank account, payouts, cash flow or owner statements. Every bookkeeper keeps the company's categories whatever is ticked; pointing a category at an account of the chart needs Company-wide or this box.
- "Company reports show the whole company": their company reports (profit and loss, and with Company-wide the cash flow and owner statements) carry every property's money instead of only their ticked properties'. Their other pages still show only their properties.
- "Sees the monthly property report on screen": the monthly report on their Reports page, for their ticked properties only.
- "Show lease start and end dates": the start and end date of each lease on their rent roll. It's off unless you tick it. They never see the lease itself or its documents.

Every box is off unless you tick it.

You can give a bookkeeper the Company-wide box, the whole-company ledger or whole-company reports, and so can a team member who manages your team if they have company-wide access themselves (every property, with "View books" on). The monthly report can be given only by someone who sees it themselves.

A property you add to the company later starts unticked for every bookkeeper, so nobody gains a new park's money without you choosing it. The bookkeeper's row on your Team page lists what they keep, and "Change access" lets you tick or untick any box at any time. The change takes effect on their next click, with no new sign-in needed.

If you sell a property or move it to another company, it comes off every bookkeeper's ticked list for the old company. Each bookkeeper who had it keeps read-only access to its history for the days it belonged to the company, and sees nothing after the transfer date. That stays true even when it was their only property, but then the whole-company ledger and whole-company reports boxes end with it: those are for a bookkeeper who keeps a property. Their row on the Team page shows the sold property and the last day of its history. A bookkeeper who never had it (one hired after the sale, say) never sees its history; with Company-wide and every property you still own ticked, they keep the books for the whole company you own today.
